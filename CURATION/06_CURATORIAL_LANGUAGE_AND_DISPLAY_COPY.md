# Curatorial Language & Display Copy
## Editorial standard for the Etherealism Photography Sphere

### The archive as a collection of distinct worlds

The portfolio should read as one photographer's archive, not as a set of unrelated branded microsites. Each album receives a specific identity, while all titles follow a disciplined editorial standard: concise, particular, memorable, and restrained.

### Album-title rationale

- **The Measure of Space** gives architecture a conceptual centre without turning it into generic luxury language.
- **Silver / Shadow** gives the parent monochrome collection a compact tonal identity.
- **Mermaid — A Fable in Silver** retains the original series name and places it clearly within the monochrome archive.
- **Cosmito — The Surface of Light** presents one commercial commission as a unified campaign with two photographic editions.
- **Elring — The Human Detail** keeps the project name visible while acknowledging the human point of view present in the folder.
- **Melek — A Study in Motion** uses photographic language rather than fitness-marketing language.
- **IYED — Notes on Presence** gives the portrait sequence an editorial identity without inventing a biography.
- **Lilith — In Her Own Light** places the subject at the centre without imposing a backstory.
- **Nighthawks — The City Thins** evokes the nocturnal register without inventing a location or event.
- **Outside the Sequence** treats the root-level photographs as deliberate independent works, not miscellaneous files.
- **Skate Up — The Ground in Motion** names the parent collection while leaving subject-led albums to carry their own identities.
- **Mael — A Different Gravity** suggests the suspended instant without naming an unverified trick.
- **Willy — Moonwalk / The Great Descent** draws the album identity from two distinctive names already present in the source folder.
- **Yan — Between Air and Ground** conveys the movement-led register without claiming what any numbered frame depicts.
- **Theatre of Shadows** preserves the strongest existing identity in the theatrical folder.
- **Between Us — Youthful Exuberance** retains the original series name while making the relationship between the two figures central.

### Individual-title standard

A photograph title should name a visual idea, give a distinctive moment within a sequence, or preserve an original title where it already carries identity. Avoid repetitive numbering, generic superlatives, or overworked metaphors. A title should not pretend to describe a scene that has not been checked.

### Caption standard

A caption is one considered sentence. It should add an idea rather than repeat the title. Avoid empty praise and generic formulations. It should help the visitor enter the work without dictating its meaning. Captions in this edition are individualised across all 81 photographs; where the image itself could not be viewed, the writing stays within the verified project or album context.

### Four distinct content fields

- **Display title:** authored editorial identity.
- **Caption:** brief curatorial interpretation.
- **Alt text:** factual accessibility description of the visible image.
- **Source filename:** immutable technical identifier.

Do not conflate these fields. Do not invent dates, locations, people, clients, products, or events.

### Review status

The Youthful Exuberance sequence has been visually reviewed. Most other large originals could not be rendered by the connected GitHub image reader in this pass. Their titles and captions are grounded in confirmed folder/project context and the previously documented archive relationships; they are not represented as a frame-by-frame visual assessment. A final contact-sheet review remains the correct approval step before public launch.
