# A Filmmaker's Eye, a Photographer's World
## Portfolio editorial direction

### The central recommendation

Build the portfolio as an **editorial archive of seeing**, not as a conventional grid of deliverables. Each body of work should feel deliberately selected, titled, and paced. Album covers act as invitations; image sequences unfold with the rhythm of a photo essay; individual photographs are allowed to stand alone.

The experience should retain the cinematic restraint of the existing visual-art-direction document while giving the photography section enough warmth, colour, and variation to feel alive. The portfolio must not force every image into one dark, blue-toned visual treatment.

### One author, two visual registers

**1. The filmmaker's register — stillness, atmosphere, precision**

Use this for film projects, film stills, posters, cinematography credits, and production-related work.

- Deep, restrained backgrounds and carefully controlled contrast.
- Typography with the patience of opening and closing film titles.
- Generous negative space and a clear hierarchy of title, role, year, and credits.
- Project imagery presented in considered sequences rather than as a busy collage.
- Motion that feels like a deliberate camera move or a cut: slow, purposeful, and never decorative.

**2. The photographer's register — colour, curiosity, human presence**

Use this for photographic albums, portraits, commercial studies, theatre, architecture, skate photography, and *Youthful Exuberance*.

- Let each collection retain its own colour temperature and emotional character.
- Use brighter or more neutral gallery surfaces when they help the photographs breathe.
- Allow asymmetrical layouts, varied image scales, and occasional full-bleed frames.
- Keep captions short and editorial. The photograph remains the primary voice.
- Avoid applying a universal colour grade or overlay to images that were made for different purposes.

These registers are not separate brands. They are two expressions of the same visual author: measured observation in cinema, and direct curiosity in photography.

### Editorial architecture

1. **Opening / presence:** introduce the filmmaker with the portrait and a concise statement of practice.
2. **Selected moving-image work:** give the film projects a clear, cinematic presentation.
3. **Photography:** present the albums as a curated index, with each cover opening a distinct visual world.
4. **Single frames:** give standalone photographs an independent space rather than hiding them inside unrelated albums.
5. **About / process:** connect the work to the person, craft, and way of seeing behind it.
6. **Contact:** finish simply, without a theatrical call-to-action.

### Album presentation

Each album should contain:

- A strong cover image.
- A refined album title and, where useful, a short subtitle.
- A one- or two-sentence editorial note.
- A paced image sequence, not an automatic masonry dump.
- Individual display titles only where they add meaning.
- Original photographer / client / collaborator credits where supplied and verified.

For the Framer CMS, keep separate fields for **canonical asset filename**, **display title**, **album**, **sequence**, **alt text**, and **credits**. This prevents creative titles from overwriting source-file identity.

### Visual and interaction rules

- Keep the overall site restrained; allow photographic collections to introduce their own colour.
- Use image-led transitions and restrained fades rather than large interface animations.
- Do not apply a heavy grain layer, blue glow, vignette, or dark overlay over every photograph.
- Use full-width images selectively as punctuation: a strong opening, a change of chapter, or a standalone image.
- On mobile, preserve the intended image order and avoid excessive cropping.
- Honour reduced-motion preferences and provide useful keyboard and screen-reader navigation.

### Asset integrity

The repository's original files remain canonical. The companion title map proposes on-page names without changing source paths. Physical file renaming should happen only after the photographs have been visually checked and any existing Framer / CMS references have been verified.

**Editorial test:** does the sequence help the visitor understand not only what was photographed, but how the photographer sees?
